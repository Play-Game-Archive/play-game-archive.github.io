Floor Games

地板游戏

H. G. Wells 赫伯特·乔治·威尔斯 · 1911 · 2026汉译

简介

威尔斯把家里的地板变成海洋、群岛和两座相邻的城市。木板叠成山丘，花园里的枝条插进小孔成为树林，积木、铅制人物与发条火车组成可以不断改造的世界。书先讲材料和尺寸，再展示探索群岛、营造城镇、搭山地铁路与弹珠塔的具体玩法；照片和页边插画记录了这些布置，也延续着他对玩具商、城市生活与成人权威的调侃。

这些游戏允许布置、故事和规则一起生长。两个孩子各有自己的辖区，共享铁道，举行市长选举，也会在大人离开后拆掉商店、另建车站，让原先的安排转向新的事件。威尔斯由此讨论成品玩具的局限、材料能否反复组合，以及儿童从造世界的过程中获得怎样的想象空间。最后一节简短谈到战争游戏，把更系统的战斗规则留给另一部书。

第一节　应备的玩具

Section I

第一节

THE TOYS TO HAVE

应备的玩具

9 THE TOYS TO HAVE 9 应备的玩具

The jolliest indoor games for boys and girls demand a floor, and the home that has no floor upon which games may be played falls so far short of happiness. It must be a floor covered with linoleum or cork carpet, so that toy soldiers and such-like will stand up upon it, and of a color and surface that will take and show chalk marks; the common green-colored cork carpet without a 10pattern is the best of all. It must be no highway to other rooms, and well lit and airy. Occasionally, alas! it must be scrubbed—and then a truce to Floor Games. Upon such a floor may be made an infinitude of imaginative games, not only keeping boys and girls happy for days together, but building up a framework of spacious and inspiring ideas in them for after life. The men of to-morrow will gain new strength from nursery floors. I am going to tell of some of these games and what is most 11needed to play them; I have tried them all and a score of others like them with my sons, and all of the games here illustrated have been set out by us. I am going to tell of them here because I think what we have done will interest other fathers and mothers, and perhaps be of use to them (and to uncles and such-like tributary subspecies of humanity) in buying presents for their own and other people's children.

男孩女孩们玩得最开心的室内游戏，都少不了一块地板；家里若没有可供游戏的地板，幸福也就大打折扣。这块地板须铺上油毡或软木地毯，让玩具兵之类的东西站得稳；它的颜色和表面，还得容得下粉笔的印迹，并使它们显现出来。最普通的绿色软木地毯，不带10任何花纹，就是最好的选择。这里不能是通往其他房间的必经之路，还要光线充足、空气流通。唉，偶尔它总得擦洗一下——地板游戏便只好暂时停战。在这样的地板上，可以开展无穷无尽的想象游戏；它们不但让男孩女孩们一连几天快快乐乐，还为他们日后的生活搭起一个宽广而富于启发的观念框架。明日的人们，将从儿童房的地板上获得新的力量。我准备讲讲其中的一些游戏，以及玩这些游戏时最11需要的东西。我和儿子们试过这里的每一种游戏，还试过二十来种同类游戏；本书图示的各个场景，也都由我们亲手布置。我之所以要在这里讲述，是因为我觉得，我们做过的事情会使别的父母感兴趣；当他们为自己的孩子或别人的孩子买礼物时，或许也能派上用场——对叔叔舅舅这类人类的附属亚种，也一样。

Now, the toys we play with time after time, and in a thousand permutations and combi12nations, belong to four main groups. We have (1) Soldiers, and with these I class sailors, railway porters, civilians, and the lower animals generally, such as I will presently describe in greater detail; (2) Bricks; (3) Boards and Planks; and (4) a lot of Clockwork Railway Rolling-Stock and Rails. Also there are certain minor objects—tin ships, Easter eggs, and the like—of which I shall make incidental mention, that like the kiwi and the duck-billed platypus refuse to be classified.

说起来，那些让我们一玩再玩、做出上千种排列组12合的玩具，可以分成四大类。我们有：（1）玩具兵；水兵、铁路搬运工、平民，以及通常所谓的低等动物，我也都归在这一类，稍后还要说得更细些；（2）积木；（3）木板和木条；（4）一大批发条铁路的车辆和轨道。此外还有若干小东西，譬如铁皮船、复活节彩蛋之类，我会顺便提到。它们就像几维鸟和鸭嘴兽，偏偏不肯归入任何一类。

13 These we arrange and rearrange in various ways upon our floor, making a world of them. In doing so we have found out all sorts of pleasant facts, and also many undesirable possibilities; and very probably our experience will help a reader here and there to the former and save him from the latter.

13我们在地板上用种种办法把它们摆了又摆，组成一个世界。这样做，让我们发现了各式各样令人愉快的事情，也发现了不少不大可取的可能性。我们的经验，很可能帮助这里的一些读者享受到前者，同时免去后者的麻烦。

For instance, our planks and boards, and what one can do with them, have been a great discovery. Lots of boys and girls seem to be quite without planks and boards at all, and there is no regular trade in 14them. The toyshops, we found, did not keep anything of the kind we wanted, and our boards, which we had to get made by a carpenter, are the basis of half the games we play. The planks and boards we have are of various sizes. We began with three of two yards by one; they were made with cross pieces like small doors; but these we found unnecessarily large, and we would not get them now after our present experience. The best thickness, we think, is an inch for the larger sizes and three-quarters 15 and a half inch for the smaller; and the best sizes are a yard square, thirty inches square, two feet, and eighteen inches square—one or two of each, and a greater number of smaller ones, 18 × 9, 9 × 9, and 9 × 4½. With the larger ones we make islands and archipelagos on our floor while the floor is a sea, or we make a large island or a couple on the Venice pattern, or we pile the smaller on the larger to make hills when the floor is a level plain, or they roof in railway stations or serve as bridges, in such manner as I will 16presently illustrate. And these boards of ours pass into our next most important possession, which is our box of bricks.

比如说，我们的木条和木板，以及用它们能够做的事情，就是一项大发现。许多男孩女孩似乎根本没有木条和木板，也似乎没有专门经营14它们的生意。我们发现，玩具店里没有我们想要的这类东西。那些不得不请木匠专门做的木板，却成了我们一半游戏的根基。我们的木条和木板有各种尺寸。最初买了三块，长两码、宽一码，装着横档，活像几扇小门；后来却发现，它们大得没有必要。有了如今的经验，我们不会再买那样的板子了。我们觉得，较大的板子以一英寸厚为最好，较小的则用四分之三英寸15和半英寸两种厚度；大小则以一码见方、三十英寸见方、两英尺见方和十八英寸见方为好——每种一两块，另备更多小块，尺寸为18×9、9×9、9×4½英寸。地板当海时，我们就用大木板做出岛屿和群岛；也可以照威尼斯的样子，做成一座大岛或相连的两座岛。地板当平原时，就把小板叠在大板上，造出山丘。它们还可以给火车站做屋顶，或者搭成桥梁；这些用法，我16马上会配图说明。谈完木板，自然就接到我们下一件最重要的家当：一箱积木。

(But I was nearly forgetting to tell this, that all the thicker and larger of these boards have holes bored through them. At about every four inches is a hole, a little larger than an ordinary gimlet hole. These holes have their uses, as I will tell later, but now let me get on to the box of bricks.)

（不过我差点忘记说，所有较厚、较大的木板上都钻了孔。大约每隔四英寸便有一孔，比普通手钻钻出的孔稍大些。这些孔自有用途，稍后我会讲到；眼下还是先接着说那箱积木。）

This, again, wasn't a toyshop acquisition. It came to us by gift from two generous 17friends, unhappily growing up and very tall at that; and they had it from parents who were one of several families who shared in the benefit of a Good Uncle. I know nothing certainly of this man except that he was a Radford of Plymouth. I have never learned nor cared to learn of his commoner occupations, but certainly he was one of those shining and distinguished uncles that tower up at times above the common levels of humanity. At times, when we consider our derived and undeserved share of his in18heritance and count the joys it gives us, we have projected half in jest and half in earnest the putting together of a little exemplary book upon the subject of such exceptional men: Celebrated Uncles, it should be called; and it should stir up all who read it to some striving at least towards the glories of the avuncular crown. What this great benefactor did was to engage a deserving unemployed carpenter through an entire winter making big boxes of wooden bricks for the almost innumerable nephews and nieces with 19which an appreciative circle of brothers and sisters had blessed him. There are whole bricks 4½ inches × 2¼ × 1⅛; there are half bricks 2¼ × 2¼ × 1⅛; and there are quarters—called by those previous owners (who have now ascended to, we hope but scarcely believe, a happier life near the ceiling) "piggys." You note how these sizes fit into the sizes of our boards, and of each size—we have never counted them, but we must have hundreds. We can pave a dozen square yards of floor with them.

这同样不是从玩具店买来的。它是两位慷慨的17朋友送给我们的；可惜他们已长大，个子还长得很高。他们又是从父母那里得到的，而他们父母的一家，则是蒙一位“好叔叔”施惠的几个家庭之一。关于这位先生，我只确知他是普利茅斯Radford家族的一员。我从未打听、也不在意他平日里做些什么；但他无疑属于那种光彩夺目、卓尔不群的叔叔——这样的人，偶尔会在人类的平常水准之上高高耸起。有时，我们想到辗转继承来、本不配享有的这份遗18产，细数它带给我们的欢乐，便半开玩笑、半当真地筹划，要为这些非凡人物编一本可作榜样的小书，题名就叫《著名的叔叔们》。它该使每一个读到它的人，至少也努力向那顶叔辈桂冠的荣光靠近一些。这位大恩人做过的事，是雇用一名值得帮助的失业木匠，让他用整整一个冬天，为几乎数不清的侄儿侄女制作一大箱一大箱的木积木；这些孩子，正是他那一圈心怀感激的兄弟姐妹19赐给他的。整块积木的尺寸是4½×2¼×1⅛英寸；半块是2¼×2¼×1⅛英寸；还有四分之一块。先前的主人把后者叫做“小猪”——那些主人如今已经长到更靠近天花板的地方，过起一种但愿更幸福的生活，虽然我们希望如此，却很难真心相信。你会发现，这些尺寸恰好与我们木板的尺寸相配。每一种我们都有好几百块吧，虽然从未真正数过。它们足以在地板上铺满十二平方码。

20 How utterly we despise the silly little bricks of the toy-shops! They are too small to make a decent home for even the poorest lead soldiers, even if there were hundreds of them, and there are never enough, never nearly enough; even if you take one at a time and lay it down and say, "This is a house," even then there are not enough. We see rich people, rich people out of motor cars, rich people beyond the dreams of avarice, going into toyshops and buying these skimpy, sickly, ridiculous pseudo-boxes 21of bricklets, because they do not know what to ask for, and the toyshops are just the merciless mercenary enemies of youth and happiness—so far, that is, as bricks are concerned. Their unfortunate under-parented offspring mess about with these gifts, and don't make very much of them, and put them away; and you see their consequences in after life in the weakly-conceived villas and silly suburbs that people have built all round big cities. Such poor under-nourished nurseries must needs 22 fall back upon the Encyclopædia Britannica, and even that is becoming flexible on India paper! But our box of bricks almost satisfies. With our box of bricks we can scheme and build, all three of us, for the best part of the hour, and still have more bricks in the box.

20我们多么看不起玩具店里那些愚蠢的小积木！它们太小，就算有好几百块，也不足以给最穷的铅制玩具兵盖一所像样的房子。何况数量从来不够，远远不够；即便你一次拿一块放下，说“这算是一所房子”，它们也还是不够。我们看见有钱人，从汽车里走下来的有钱人，富得连贪心鬼都做梦也想不到的有钱人，走进玩具店，买下那些少得可怜、病恹恹、可笑得简直配不上积木箱之名的小木块盒子21，只因为他们不知道该要什么。至于玩具店，就积木而言，实在是青春和幸福那毫不留情、唯利是图的敌人。他们那些可怜的、没得到父母得当照顾的孩子，拿着礼物胡乱摆弄一番，没弄出什么名堂，便收了起来。你会在日后的生活中看到这种后果：大城市四周，那些构想软弱的别墅和傻乎乎的郊区，就是人们这样造出来的。如此可怜、养料不足的儿童房，不免只好22求助于《大英百科全书》；可是连它如今也用印度纸印得软绵绵了！不过，我们这箱积木已差不多令人满意。我们三个人可以一起设计、搭建上大半个钟头，箱子里还留着更多积木。

So much now for the bricks. I will tell later how we use cartridge paper and cardboard and other things to help in our building, and of the decorative use we make of plasticine. Of course, it goes without say23ing that we despise those foolish, expensive, made-up wooden and pasteboard castles that are sold in shops—playing with them is like playing with somebody else's dead game in a state of rigor mortis. Let me now say a little about toy soldiers and the world to which they belong. Toy soldiers used to be flat, small creatures in my own boyhood, in comparison with the magnificent beings one can buy to-day. There has been an enormous improvement in our national physique in this respect. Now 24 they stand nearly two inches high and look you broadly in the face, and they have the movable arms and alert intelligence of scientifically exercised men. You get five of them mounted or nine afoot in a box for a small price. We three like those of British manufacture best; other makes are of incompatible sizes, and we have a rule that saves much trouble, that all red coats belong to G. P. W., and all other colored coats to F. R. W., all gifts, bequests, and accidents notwithstanding. Also we have sailors; 25but, since there are no red-coated sailors, blue counts as red.

积木先说到这里。稍后我会讲，我们怎样用厚绘图纸、纸板和其他东西辅助搭建，又怎样发挥橡皮泥的装饰作用。不言23而喻，我们也瞧不起商店里出售的那些愚蠢、昂贵、事先搭好的木头或硬纸板城堡——玩那些东西，就像在玩别人已经死去、陷入尸僵的游戏。现在，再说说玩具兵和它们所属的世界吧。与今天可以买到的那些气派家伙相比，我小时候的玩具兵又扁又小。从这个角度看，我们国民的体格实在有了巨大的进步。如今24它们高近两英寸，大大方方地正视着你；手臂可以活动，神气机敏，就像受过科学锻炼的人。一盒五个骑兵或九个步兵，花不多的钱便买得到。我们三个人最喜欢英国制造的；别处制造的尺寸不相配。我们还有一条省掉许多麻烦的规矩：红制服一概归G. P. W.，其他颜色的制服一概归F. R. W.，不论礼物、遗赠或偶然情况如何，都是如此。我们也有水兵；25不过水兵没有穿红制服的，所以蓝色就算红色。

Then we have "beefeaters," [1 The warders in the Tower of London are called "beefeaters"; the origin of the term is obscure.] Indians, Zulus, for whom there are special rules. We find we can buy lead dogs, cats, lions, tigers, horses, camels, cattle, and elephants of a reasonably corresponding size, and we have also several boxes of railway porters, and some soldiers we bought in Hesse-Darmstadt that we pass off on an unsuspecting home world as policemen. But we want civilians very badly. We found a box of German 26 civilians once in a shop, the right size but rather heavy, and running to nearly five cents apiece (which is too dear), gentlemen in tweed suits carrying bags, a top-hatted gentleman, ladies in gray and white, two children, and a dog, and so on, but we have never been able to find any more. They do not seem to be made at all—will toy manufacturers please note? I write now as if I were Consul-General in Toyland, noting new opportunities for trade. Consequent upon this dearth, our little world suffers 27from an exaggerated curse of militarism, and even the grocer wears epaulettes. This might please Lord Roberts and Mr. Leo Maxse, but it certainly does not please us. I wish, indeed, that we could buy boxes of tradesmen: a blue butcher, a white baker with a loaf of standard bread, a merchant or so; boxes of servants, boxes of street traffic, smart sets, and so forth.
